Smart Home Add-ons: what actually matters

The accessory itself is usually simple — a plug, a bulb, a sensor. The thing that determines whether it's still useful in two years is which ecosystem it locks you into. Here's what to check first.

Protocol

Wi-Fi devices are simplest to set up but load your router. Zigbee/Z-Wave devices need a separate hub but handle many devices more reliably. Matter is an emerging standard aiming to unify both.

App ecosystem

Check whether a device works with the voice assistant and app you already use (Alexa, Google Home, Apple Home) before buying — not all devices support all three.

Local vs. cloud control

Some devices stop working entirely if the manufacturer's cloud servers go down or the company shuts down support. Local-control options are more resilient long-term.

Wi-Fi vs. Zigbee vs. Matter

Wi-Fi smart plugs and bulbs connect directly to your router — easiest to set up, but every device adds load to your home network, which can slow things down once you have a dozen or more. Zigbee and Z-Wave devices instead connect to a separate hub, which handles a large number of devices more efficiently, at the cost of buying that extra hub. Matter is a newer standard several manufacturers are adopting specifically to let devices work across ecosystems without needing separate hubs for each brand — worth checking for on newer product listings.

Check ecosystem compatibility before buying

Not every smart device works with every voice assistant. A plug that only supports its own proprietary app, without Alexa, Google Home, or Apple Home integration, means you're stuck opening a separate app just to turn on a lamp. Check the listing specifically for which ecosystems are supported — "smart" alone doesn't guarantee it talks to the assistant you already use.

Local control vs. cloud dependency

Some budget smart devices route every command through the manufacturer's cloud servers, which means the device stops responding if those servers have an outage — or stops working permanently if the company shuts down. Devices that support local control (working over your home network even without internet) are more resilient, though this feature isn't always advertised clearly and may require checking community reviews or forums.
