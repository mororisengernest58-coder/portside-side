Cables & Wall Chargers: what actually matters

Most cables look identical in a product photo. The differences that actually affect your day — charge speed, how long the cable survives being run over by a desk chair, whether it works with your specific charger — are buried in the spec sheet. Here's what to check before you buy, plus the picks we'd point a friend toward.

Wattage

Match the cable and charger's wattage to your device. A phone charges fine on 20W; a laptop usually needs 65–100W to charge at full speed.

Cable material

Braided nylon resists fraying at the connector far longer than plain silicone or PVC, especially where the cable bends at your desk edge.

Port standard

USB-C has replaced Lightning on most new devices. Check what your specific phone, tablet, and laptop actually use before ordering a multi-pack.

Wattage: the number that decides charge speed

A charger's wattage is the ceiling, not a guarantee — your device and cable both have to support it too. A 65W charger paired with a cable only rated for 60W will bottleneck at 60W. If you're charging a laptop, check the manufacturer's minimum recommended wattage first; going under it means the laptop may charge slowly or not at all while in heavy use.

For phones, anything from 20W to 45W is typically enough to reach fast-charge speeds. For tablets and most modern laptops, look for 65W or higher, and 100W if you want one charger to cover a laptop, a phone, and a set of earbuds at once via multiple ports.

Cable material and durability

The connector — where the cable meets the plug — is almost always where cables fail first, not the middle of the cord. Braided nylon sheathing holds up noticeably better under repeated bending than smooth silicone or bare PVC, particularly if the cable gets pulled at an angle (charging in bed, running off the edge of a desk). Reinforced strain-relief at the connector is a better predictor of lifespan than the advertised "bend test" number on the packaging.

Port standard: check before you multi-pack

USB-C has become the standard across most new phones, tablets, and laptops, with Lightning increasingly limited to older devices. Before buying a multi-pack, confirm what ports your specific devices use — mixing up USB-C-to-C and USB-C-to-Lightning is the most common return reason in this category.
