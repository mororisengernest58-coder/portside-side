Laptop Stands & Sleeves: what actually matters

A stand's job is simple — get your screen to eye level and let air move underneath — but the details that make one comfortable for eight hours a day versus annoying after twenty minutes aren't obvious from a photo.

Height & angle

Look for adjustable height, not just a fixed incline — eye level depends on your monitor setup and chair height, which varies person to person.

Material & airflow

Aluminum and mesh designs let more air reach the laptop's underside vents than solid plastic risers, which matters for sustained workloads.

Portability

Foldable stands trade some stability for packing flat in a bag; rigid stands are sturdier but stay on the desk.

Height and ergonomics

The point of a laptop stand is getting the screen to roughly eye level so you're not hunching forward — but "eye level" depends on your desk, chair, and whether you're using an external keyboard. A stand with multiple height settings adapts to more setups than a single fixed angle. If you're not pairing the stand with an external keyboard and mouse, raising the screen just moves the strain from your neck to your wrists — plan for both together.

Material and cooling

Laptops vent heat from the bottom and sides, and a solid plastic or wood riser can block that airflow more than an aluminum stand with an open frame or mesh cutouts. If you regularly run demanding workloads (video editing, compiling code, gaming), airflow matters more than it does for someone mostly in a browser or word processor.

Portability trade-offs

Foldable, lightweight stands pack flat for a bag but usually flex more under the laptop's weight, especially at the top of the height range. Rigid all-metal stands are more stable and often better for cooling, but they're built to live on one desk rather than travel. Decide which you actually need before optimizing for one over the other.
